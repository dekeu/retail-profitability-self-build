# Retail profitability findings

I used the fictional Contoso dataset, filtered to 2025 USD transactions and all categories. At baseline, revenue was $12,176,250.37 and gross profit was $6,817,301.33, giving a gross margin of 56.0%. All price, unit-cost and volume assumptions were zero unless stated below.

## 1. Computers contributed the most gross profit

Computers generated $2,460,913.02 in gross profit, or 36.1% of the total. It also had the highest revenue at $4,363,034.07. Because this category accounts for more than a third of gross profit, it is a useful starting point for a closer review of product performance and costs. My next question is whether that contribution comes from a few products or is spread across the category.

## 2. The highest-margin category had a smaller sales base

Music, Movies and Audio Books had the highest gross margin at 58.5%, compared with 56.4% for Computers. Its revenue was $926,515.70 and its gross profit was $541,952.28. Computers therefore contributed much more gross profit despite having a lower margin. Looking at revenue, gross profit and margin together helped me see both the size of each category and how much it retained after product costs. I would check demand and the products within each category before deciding where to focus growth.

## 3. A 5% price increase raised modelled gross profit by $608,812.52

With Price at 5%, Unit cost at 0% and Volume at 0%, the planner showed gross profit of $7,426,113.85. That is $608,812.52, or about 8.9%, above baseline. Gross margin rose from 56.0% to 58.1%. This shows the effect of higher prices when sales volume and unit costs stay unchanged. The next step would be to use the volume control to see how much of the gain remains if sales fall. The model does not predict how customers would respond to the price increase.

## What these findings cover

These results describe fictional sample data. Gross profit includes product costs but excludes operating expenses and tax. The planner applies the same percentage changes across the selected products, keeps product mix constant and does not account for inventory limits or customer demand.

Sources: the project's 2025 category and yearly SQL reference outputs, and the checked Tableau scenario recorded in [test_log.md](test_log.md). Margin percentages and the gross-profit share are rounded to one decimal place.
